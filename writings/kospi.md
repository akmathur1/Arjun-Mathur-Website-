On October 15, Korean brokerages sold 80.5 billion won of their customers' shares without asking them. A week earlier the daily figure had been 6.4 billion. Between the two, the KOSPI fell seven straight sessions, from about 6,900 to 5,500, and the margin loans underneath it, above 34 trillion won on October 13 for the first time since July, began shrinking the only way they shrink quickly: by being called.

The run-up needs dates. The KOSPI first closed above 2,000 in July 2007. It ended 2024 at 2,399, with a year above 3,000 in 2021. Seventeen years, four hundred points, and a literature on the Korea discount.

Then it closed above 4,000 last October, 5,000 in January, 6,000 in February, 7,000 in May, 8,000 three weeks later, 9,000 in June, and touched 9,385 on June 19. Seven thousand points in eighteen months. It then fell 44 percent in six weeks, to 5,263 on July 29, recovered to 7,100 by September, and gave up a fifth again in the first half of October. It closed Wednesday at 6,012 and Thursday at 5,938. That is not the chart of a stock market. It is the chart of a margin account.

The earnings are real, which is the dangerous part. SK Hynix reported this week: revenue of 104.6 trillion won, up 328 percent, an operating margin of 79.7 percent, memory sold out through 2029. Samsung made 107 trillion won of operating profit in the same quarter and promised 90 to 110 trillion won of dividends and buybacks. Two companies sit atop an index in a country where more than half of last quarter's growth surprise came from chips, private consumption fell at its fastest rate in four and a half years, and bank delinquencies are at a ten-year high. The index is the memory cycle. The country is not.

The retail investor's response was to borrow. Margin loans rose from 28.9 trillion won at the end of July to 34.3 trillion by October 12, up nearly a fifth, while cash in brokerage accounts fell 7 percent to 96.9 trillion. A market without leverage damps its own fluctuations, because a lower price finds a buyer. A market with 34 trillion won of margin amplifies them, because a lower price finds a margin clerk.

Forced sales rose 12.7-fold in a week, unsettled receivables reached 1.24 trillion won, and the margin balance dropped almost a trillion in a single day. It has since settled below 30 trillion, and on October 27 retail investors net sold 949 billion won while foreigners bought. The forced selling did what the dip could not: it broke the loop, for now.

The government has not been a bystander. On Thursday the president's senior secretary for policy apologized in the National Assembly for single-stock leveraged ETFs, products paying twice a stock's daily move, whose launch, he said, coincided with a period of repricing. Daily double leverage in a market that moves 6 percent on a Wednesday turns volatility into losses even when the stock ends where it began, and the state waved it through.

So, a reset. I will not name a date, only what has already happened: the index has been cut by 44 percent once and 20 percent once in four months, and the forced selling that did the second cut began with margin debt at 34 trillion won. The next leg begins wherever the margin balance sits when memory prices stop rising, and memory prices have never not stopped rising. Hynix says the shortage runs through 2030. The whole trade is a bet on that word, through.

The photograph above these stories is usually the same one: a wall of screens in the dealing room at Hana Bank's head office in Jung-gu, showing the KOSPI and the KOSDAQ. The screens show the index. They have never shown the loans.
